\sectionline{Quick summary}
I compared our frozen W37 BIO predictions with Alex's W40--41 integrated QA predictions \emph{question by question}, rather than relying on two unrelated sets of summary statistics. The integrated QA model did better on both answer extraction and deciding when to return nothing. Its normalized exact match increased from 42.60\% to 55.84\%, while answerability macro-F1 increased from 0.6004 to 0.8550. The main practical improvement was fewer false abstentions: 282 in W37 versus 39 in W40--41. However, W37 was still uniquely correct on 63 questions, and both models failed exact match on 447.

\sectionline{1. How the comparison was done}
We aligned W37's \texttt{outputs/w37\_bio\_seed123\_original.csv} and Alex's seed-44 \texttt{validation\_predictions.csv} using their validation IDs. All 1,155 IDs matched one-to-one, with identical language, question, context, gold answer, gold offset and gold answerability label. Both models therefore face exactly the same examples. We compare \emph{SQuAD-style normalized} exact match and answer-token text F1, and macro-F1 of binary answerability. W37's required BIO \emph{micro-F1 over context tokens} is a different metric (41.44\%) and must not be confused with SQuAD token F1 (48.56\%).

Alex's report also presents mean and standard deviation from W40--41 seeds 42, 43 and 44. This matched comparison uses only the saved \emph{seed-44} predictions; it does not pretend that the W37 seed-123 run and the W40--41 seed-44 run have identical training/selection procedures.

The most common distinction in these examples is between \emph{finding a related phrase} and \emph{answering the specific question}. Wrong entities and overly long spans are different failures from false abstention or giving an unsupported answer. One caution: the Arabic Paul example involves a distinction between ``an apostle'' and ``one of the Twelve'', so its semantics deserve careful wording even though the gold span is valid.

\sectionline{My two cents and limitations}
The integrated model is clearly better on these matched validation questions, especially at avoiding missed answers, but this is not evidence that it always wins: W37 gets 63 examples right that W40--41 misses. We have \emph{not} established why the architectures differ internally, and some exact-match failures are boundary/granularity issues rather than entirely wrong meanings. Alex selected the integrated QA checkpoint and its no-answer threshold on validation; these are development results, not a held-out test. For the final report we should show the three-seed W40--41 summary alongside this clearly labelled seed-44 paired inspection, and condense the representative errors without dropping the analysis.
